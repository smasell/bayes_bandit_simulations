# Bayesian bandit: historical day-7 revenue, 15% lift

Under the simulated policy, allow roughly **82,000 total users** for an 80%
chance of stopping with price 100 as the winner, or **104,000 total users**
for a 90% chance. These are counts across all four prices, with unequal allocation.

| Correct winner declared by this user count | 15% higher payer spending | 15% more payers |
|---|---:|---:|
| 50% of runs | 46,000 | 48,000 |
| 80% of runs | 82,000 | 82,000 |
| 90% of runs | 104,000 | 102,000 |

Each scenario was simulated 2,000 times, with a 300,000-user cap. Wrong selections
and unfinished runs are counted as failures when computing completion quantiles.
At the cap, correct selections occurred in 99.9% and 99.8% of the lift runs.
Finite simulation means the quantiles are estimates, not exact guarantees.

## Historical data and constructed scenarios

The source has 113,771 rows, interpreted as individual users with completed
day-7 revenue, including non-payers. Mean revenue is 2.43952, standard deviation
12.69294, and 94.9794% of users have zero revenue. Prices 70, 80, and 90 share
this empirical distribution. Price 100 has mean 2.80545 in both lift scenarios.
Positive outcomes are resampled from the historical positive observations.
The lift is generated by either multiplying positive revenue by 1.15 or
increasing the payer probability from 5.0206% to 5.7737%.

## Policy and posterior

- Start with 2,000 users per price (8,000 total).
- Allocate each subsequent 2,000-user batch using Thompson sampling mixed with
  exploration: each arm has at least 5% allocation probability.
- Estimate the posterior of mean revenue using a normal approximation to the
  Bayesian bootstrap, with its exact mean and variance. This is an approximate
  Bayesian method, not exact Bayesian-bootstrap posterior sampling.
- Stop when the posterior probability that any arm has the highest mean is
  at least 95%. Use 1,024 draws for screening and 8,192 independent draws for
  confirmation near the threshold.

For the spending-lift scenario, mean allocation at stop was approximately 7,774,
7,873, 8,029, and 30,609 users for prices 70, 80, 90, and 100. These are averages
over stopped runs, not fixed per-arm targets.

## Error check and interpretation

When all four arms had the same true revenue, 107 of 2,000 runs (5.35%) declared
a false winner by 300,000 users. Monte Carlo standard error for that rate is
approximately 0.5 percentage points. A posterior threshold is not a guarantee
of frequentist error control with repeated stopping.

The rule establishes evidence that an arm is best; it does not establish that
its lift is at least 15%. The 15% lift is the assumed truth in the simulation.
The previous fixed-experiment estimate used different decision criteria, so
the sample counts are not an equal-error comparison of the two methods.

All batch outcomes mature before the next allocation. The estimates concern
completed seven-day observations. A live experiment with ongoing enrollment
and seven-day delayed feedback can require different allocation and additional
enrollment. Calendar duration cannot be estimated without traffic and rollout
details. No price elasticity can be inferred from this one-column CSV; real
differences among the three lower prices may change these results. Historical
distribution uncertainty, seasonality, and user dependence are not simulated.

Reproduce using `historical_bandit.py`; parameters and seeds are recorded in
`summary.json`, and milestone probabilities are in `progress.csv`.
